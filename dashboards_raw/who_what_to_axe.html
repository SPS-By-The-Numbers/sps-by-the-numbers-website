Fine print

  • Data: OSPI S-275 personnel report, Seattle Public Schools, 2024–25 final (sps-btn-data.safs_s275) — the same pull as the salary skyline. One bubble per employee; bubble area, bar heights, and medians are total compensation — total_final_salary plus the F-196-derived benefits load (see below).
  • “SEA-represented” is approximated from OSPI duty codes (teachers 31–34, substitutes 52, certificated support 39–49 & 63–64, paraeducators 91, office & clerical 94). Real bargaining-unit rosters differ at the margins. Custodial, trades and food service staff belong to other unions and are left out of both sides of the game.
  • Benefits are always included, from F-196 actuals. SPS’s 2024–25 F-196 reports $240.9M of Object 4 (Employee Benefits & Payroll Taxes) against $737.7M of salary Objects 2+3. S-275 final salaries total $684.0M, so scaling Object 4 by that ratio attributes $223.4M of benefits to S-275-covered pay — 32.7¢ per salary dollar. Every staff figure in the game — bubble areas, skyline bars, medians, and the money a cut frees — is salary × 1.327. Purchased services carry no benefits load. Source: sps-btn-data.safs_f19x.general_fund_expenditures, actuals, objects 2/3/4.
  • The dashboard skyline draws one bar per person at a single shared width and dollar scale across all sections, so section widths compare headcounts directly (which is why the central-office block is a thin strip next to 5,743 SEA staff) and bar heights compare pay. If you hand a small group an enormous pool, its blue stack can run off the top of the chart — the panel numbers stay exact.
  • With “Show program splits” on, the SEA side of the skyline is split by program, assigning each employee to the bucket holding the plurality of their assignment FTE (ties go to basic; zero-FTE staff use assignment-row plurality): LAP is OSPI program 55, Title I is programs 51–53, MLL (multilingual learners) is programs 64+65, and Special education is programs 21+24 (combined because duty-46 school psychologists moved from program 24 to 21 in 2022–23); everything else is basic ed + other. Among SEA-represented staff that gives Basic ed + other 3,408 · LAP 97 · Title I 53 · MLL 357 · Special education 1,828. Redistribution weights stay per staff group — the program split is a lens, not a control.
  • The hole is real and it recurs. The $23,706,321 budget deficit is the General Fund gap between expenditures and revenues + other financing sources in the latest F-196 actuals (2024–25, sps-btn-data.safs_f19x; the 2025–26 F-196 has not been filed yet) — a structural gap that repeats every year until something changes. The Economic Stabilization Fund shows $0 committed in every 2026–27 fund-balance column of the district’s budget book (PDF p.21); board policy floors it at 3% of the total budget, and 3% of the $1,338,853,189 General Fund expenditure budget is $40,165,596, refilled at your chosen $/yr pace until the floor is met. What the game shows is the yearly cash flow for the foreseeable future.
  • Purchased services come from the same F-196 actuals: General Fund Object 7, split into a special-ed partition (programs 21+24) and everything else, one bubble per NCES spending class sized by the summed annual amount (tools/salary_skyline/services_query.sql). The same dollars-per-area scale as the salary bubbles, so contracted student transportation ($59.7M) dwarfs any administrator. Services carry no benefits load. In the skyline they appear as area-true rectangles drawn at the block row’s average compensation height, greying with the cut fraction. Cutting a service class zeroes a whole year of that spending — judge for yourself which contracts a district could actually live without.
  • Small bubbles and short bars are usually partial-year hires, not low-paid roles.
