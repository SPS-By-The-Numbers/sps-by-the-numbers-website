1The gap is widening — and flat raises are why

A flat percentage raise gives the same proportional bump to everyone, which means a larger dollar bump to those already paid most. Across all SEA-represented staff — where an aide earns roughly half what a senior teacher does — the absolute distance between the lowest- and highest-paid keeps growing.

Flat ratio, growing dollars

From 2013–14 to 2024–25 the dollar gap between the top and bottom fifth grew from $57,303 to $83,849 (+46%). The ratio (top ÷ bottom fifth) actually narrowed, from 2.82× to 2.57× — the bottom rose faster in percentage terms (+69% vs +55% at the top), helped by state minimum-salary floors, the $1,500 low-paid bonus, and aide/office schedule gains. But the absolute dollar gap widened, because a flat percentage on a much higher base is always more dollars. Seattle's contract raises were exactly that — a single uniform percentage added to every schedule each year (7% / 4% / 3% / 2.5%; CBA §A.6), applied to all SEA units alike.

A note on grouping: this trend uses equal-count fifths (lowest-paid 20% vs highest-paid 20%), which is what tracks a widening gap over time; sections 2–3 use equal-width $20k pay bands, the natural unit for a single year's distribution and for designing bracket-by-bracket raises.

2The 2024–25 pay distribution, by salary band

All 5,398 SEA-represented staff (≥0.5 FTE) sorted into five equal-width $20k bands from $45k to $145k by actual annual pay. The bottom band (B1) is the largest — 28%, overwhelmingly paraeducators/aides and office professionals; teachers and ESAs fill the upper bands.

The two end bands are open-ended. B1 includes everyone below $65k (down to the lowest ~$36k), and B5 includes everyone at $125k and above (up to ~$172k) — so a trim on B5 in §3 applies to every staff member at $125k+, not just those under $145k.

3What a progressive redo of the 2022 contract would have looked like

The 2022–25 contract added a flat percentage to the entire salary schedule each year — 7% (2022–23), 4% (2023–24), 3% (2024–25) — confirmed verbatim in the contract (“will be added to the salary schedule,” SEA–SPS CBA, §A.6). Below, the same three raises are re-run as a progressive schedule, holding each year’s total raise pool identical to the flat version (cost-neutral to the dollar). SEA-represented staff are grouped into five equal $20k salary bands (B1 <$65k … B5 $125k+, by 2024–25 actual annual pay). Use the controls to set how much is trimmed from the top two bands — everything recomputes live.

The exchange rate: trimming the top to lift the bottom

The bottom band (B1, <$65k) is the largest — 28% of all SEA staff, overwhelmingly paraeducators/aides and office professionals; the top band (B5) holds 22%. The top band’s payroll is 1.9× the bottom band’s, so 0.5% trimmed from B5 funds ~0.95% added to B1 (one point at the top ≈ 1.9 points at the bottom), or about +0.61% spread across the bottom two bands. The trims you set below are redistributed to B1 + B2 — with a control to slope how much each gets — while the middle band (B3) stays at the flat rate.

Trims max 3.0% (the smallest annual raise); beyond that a band’s raise turns negative. The slope sets how the freed money splits between the bottom two bands, cost-neutrally.

Sources & method. OSPI S-275 personnel reporting, Seattle (CCDDD 17001), 2013–14 → 2024–25. Population: SEA-represented roles — certificated non-supervisory staff (duty roots 31–34 teachers; 41 librarian; 42 counselor; 43 OT; 44 social worker; 45 SLP; 46 psychologist; 47 nurse; 48 PT; 39 orientation/mobility), substitutes (52), paraeducators/aides (91), and office professionals/SAEOP (94) — identified by each person's is_major assignment, ≥0.5 FTE with a valid salary (5,398 staff in 2024–25). Excludes administrators, principals, and directors (non-SEA), and other-union classified staff (custodians, trades, food service, technical). Salary basis is actual annual base salary (“total final salary”), not FTE-normalized, so part-time aides and office staff sit at the bottom as they do in take-home terms; because it reflects actual payroll, part-year staff can also appear low. Section 1 uses equal-count fifths (the trend measure); sections 2–3 use five equal-width $20k pay bands ($45–145k by 2024–25 pay). The interactive redistribution re-runs the actual 2022–25 raises (7/4/3 — the same percentages applied to every SEA schedule) each year cost-neutrally, trimming the top two bands (B4, B5) by the amounts selected and reallocating the proceeds to the bottom two (B1, B2) with an adjustable B1:B2 slope; it is illustrative and not modeled against each schedule's step/lane structure. Nominal dollars, not inflation-adjusted.
